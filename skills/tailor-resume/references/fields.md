# Field conventions

Read the section for the posting's field. When a posting spans two fields (a software role at a
bank, a finance role at a tech company), take section order from the role's function and vocabulary
from the employer's industry.

Every field uses the same template, the same one-page limit, and the same writing rules. Fields
differ in section order, in whether a summary appears, and in what counts as proof.

## Shared rules

- **Students and graduates within about two years**: Education sits above Work Experience.
- **Experienced candidates**: Work Experience sits above Education, and coursework is dropped.
- **Order inside a section**: reverse chronological. Roles that overlap in time may be ordered by
  relevance to the posting.
- **Bullet formula**: verb, what was done, with what, and the measured result. Each field below
  names the results its readers look for.
- **Unrelated jobs** stay when they fill a timeline hole or prove a trait the posting asks for
  (client contact, long hours, cash handling, leadership). One or two bullets each.

## Tech

Software engineering, data, machine learning, site reliability, security, product, IT.

- **Sections**: Summary, Education, Work Experience, Projects and Leadership, Skills.
- **Summary**: two or three sentences that name the exact role title, the strongest production or
  project evidence, and the main languages.
- **Lead with** the evidence closest to the team's work. Match the base to the role type:

  | Role type | Lead evidence |
  |---|---|
  | Backend, fintech, enterprise | Production services, data integrity, testing, deployment |
  | Systems, hardware, embedded | C or C++, operating system internals, computer architecture |
  | AI, machine learning, agents | Models or agent systems shipped, evaluation, data pipelines |
  | Reliability, DevOps, infrastructure | Operations, monitoring, automation, incident handling |
  | Frontend, mobile | Shipped interfaces, performance, accessibility, user numbers |
  | Data, analytics | Pipelines, SQL, dashboards, decisions the analysis changed |
  | Generalist, large employers | Breadth of languages, fundamentals, scale |

- **Quantify**: users, requests, latency, uptime, cost, build or deploy time, test coverage, data
  volume, stars or downloads.
- **Skills block**: four to six categories named in the posting's own terms (Programming Languages,
  Backend and Data, Cloud and DevOps, Testing and Practices). List each technology with the
  posting's spelling.
- **Contact line**: add GitHub or a portfolio site when the work there is presentable.
- **Projects** are swapped per posting. A project is strongest when it is live, used by someone, or
  open source with outside contributors.
- **Next stage**: online coding assessment, then technical interviews. Tell the user which
  languages the posting names for the assessment.

## Finance

Corporate finance, financial planning and analysis, accounting, audit, asset and wealth management,
equity research, risk, commercial banking, sales and trading, quantitative roles.

- **Sections**: Education, Work Experience, Leadership and Activities, Skills and Interests.
  Experienced candidates may open with a two-sentence Summary. Students omit it.
- **Education line**: degree, major, graduation date, GPA when it is 3.5 of 4.0 or better or when
  the posting sets a minimum, honors, and finance, accounting, and statistics coursework.
- **Lead with** work that touched money, accuracy, or analysis. Reframe non-finance experience
  around the numbers handled, the controls followed, and the decisions informed.
- **Quantify**: currency amounts, percentages, basis points, budget or portfolio size, assets under
  management, forecast accuracy, variance found, days cut from a close cycle, accounts reconciled.
- **Vocabulary to mirror when the master supports it**: financial analysis, forecasting, budgeting,
  variance analysis, financial statements, reconciliation, month-end close, valuation, GAAP or
  IFRS, internal controls, risk management, portfolio analysis.
- **Skills block categories**: Technical (Excel with the specific functions used, PowerPoint, SQL,
  Python, VBA, Power BI or Tableau, Bloomberg, SAP or Oracle or NetSuite), Certifications,
  Languages, Interests.
- **Certifications** carry weight here. State the exact status: CPA candidate, CFA Level II
  candidate, FRM Part I passed. Never round a status up.
- **Quantitative and trading roles**: mathematics, statistics, and programming lead. Competitions,
  research, and market projects with a stated method and result count as evidence.
- **Tone**: conservative. No personal website unless it shows relevant work.
- **Next stage**: numerical or situational online tests and recorded video interviews are common.

## Investment banking

Investment banking, private equity, venture capital, corporate development, restructuring.

- **Sections**: Education, Work Experience, Leadership and Activities, Skills and Interests. No
  Summary. Banks read the education block first for students and analysts.
- **Education block**: school, degree, major, graduation month and year, GPA, honors, relevant
  coursework (corporate finance, financial accounting, valuation). Add SAT or ACT only when the
  score is strong and the bank is in the United States. Many banks screen at a 3.5 GPA. When the
  user's GPA is lower or absent, tell them it may screen the application out and that a referral
  matters more than the document.
- **Transaction experience** is the strongest content. For each deal give the type (sell-side
  merger, buy-side acquisition, initial public offering, debt financing, leveraged buyout), the
  size, the client's industry, and the user's own work (built the discounted cash flow model, ran
  comparable company analysis, drafted sections of the confidential information memorandum,
  supported due diligence). Describe a confidential deal without the names, for example "$250M
  sale of a healthcare services company".
- **Without deal experience**: lead with the most analytical, numbers-heavy, client-facing work.
  Valuation projects, stock pitches, case competitions, and student investment funds go under
  Leadership and Activities with the method and the result.
- **Quantify**: deal size, valuation ranges, model outputs, number of companies screened, fund size,
  returns against a benchmark, hours or deadlines met.
- **Vocabulary to mirror when the master supports it**: financial modeling, valuation, discounted
  cash flow (DCF), comparable company analysis, precedent transactions, leveraged buyout (LBO),
  mergers and acquisitions (M&A), three-statement model, accretion and dilution, pitch book, due
  diligence.
- **Skills and Interests**: Technical (Excel modeling, PowerPoint, Bloomberg, Capital IQ, FactSet),
  Certifications (CFA level, SIE, Series 79 or 63, CSC), Languages, Interests. Interests are
  expected and get read. Make them specific enough to start a conversation.
- **Format**: the most conservative of any field. No links beyond LinkedIn, no project jargon, no
  tool lists longer than one line each.
- **Cover letter**: read the banking notes in `references/cover-letter.md`. Ask the user for the
  names of people at the firm they have spoken to. Never invent a contact or a deal.
- **Next stage**: recorded video interview, then a superday with technical and fit questions.
  Recruiting for summer analyst roles opens more than a year ahead. Tell the user when a deadline
  looks early for that reason.

## Consulting

Management, strategy, economic, and technology consulting.

- **Sections**: Education, Work Experience, Leadership and Activities, Skills and Interests. No
  Summary.
- **Education block**: GPA, honors, test scores when strong, scholarships.
- **Lead with** impact and leadership. Each bullet shows a problem, the user's action, and a result
  a client or organization felt.
- **Quantify**: revenue, cost, time, people led, size of the client or budget, adoption of the
  recommendation.
- **Vocabulary to mirror when the master supports it**: analysis, recommendation, stakeholder,
  client, market sizing, workstream, presentation to senior leaders.
- **Leadership and Activities** matter as much as work experience. Elected roles, founding
  something, and competitive achievement all count.
- **Next stage**: online problem-solving tests, then case interviews. Tell the user.

## Other fields

For any field not listed, derive the conventions before drafting:

1. **Find the field's proof.** Name the numbers its readers trust.

   | Field | Proof |
   |---|---|
   | Marketing | Campaign reach, conversion, cost per acquisition, return on ad spend, audience growth |
   | Sales | Quota attainment, revenue closed, pipeline built, accounts won, ranking on the team |
   | Operations, supply chain | Cost saved, throughput, lead time, error rate, inventory accuracy |
   | Healthcare | Licenses, patient volume, settings worked, record systems used, outcomes |
   | Education | Certification, class sizes, grade levels, measured student results |
   | Engineering outside software | Licensure status, design tools, standards applied, project budgets |
   | Design, media | Portfolio link in the contact line, shipped work, audience or client results |
   | Law, policy | Admission status, practice areas, research and writing output, matters supported |
   | Research | Methods, publications, grants, instruments, datasets |
   | Nonprofit | Funds raised, people served, volunteers managed, programs launched |

2. **Take the vocabulary from the posting.** Skills categories and keywords use the posting's own
   terms.
3. **Find the gates.** Licenses, registrations, and background checks decide many of these fields.
   Put a held license in the skills block and name its status exactly.
4. **Choose the sections.** Summary, Education, Work Experience, Projects and Leadership, Skills is
   the default. Drop Summary for students in conservative fields.

## Where one page is not the norm

This skill always produces one page. Tell the user when the posting's field or country expects
something else, and let them decide:

- Academic and medical CVs run to many pages and list every publication.
- Government applications in some countries require long, form-style resumes.
- Senior executive resumes often run two pages.
- Outside the United States and Canada, a two-page CV is common and some countries expect a photo
  or personal details. This template follows United States and Canadian practice, which leaves out
  photos, age, and marital status.

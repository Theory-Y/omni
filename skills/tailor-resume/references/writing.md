# Writing style

Applies to every sentence in a resume or cover letter. The goal is text a recruiter reads as
written by the candidate.

No style passes every AI-writing detector. Specific facts in plain words are what readers and
detectors both treat as human. The user's own read-through at the end does the rest.

## Punctuation

| Banned | Rewrite |
|---|---|
| Em dash, en dash | Use a comma, or split into two sentences. |
| Semicolon | Split into two sentences, or join with "and". |
| Colon before a list | Use "including" or "such as", or put the list in a sentence of its own. |
| Colon before an explanation | Split into two sentences. |
| Exclamation mark, ellipsis | Remove. |

- Date ranges use a plain hyphen with spaces ("Jul 2026 - Present").
- The contact line and the awards line separate items with a vertical bar.
- The colon that closes a bold template label (`<b>Coursework:</b>`) is the only colon allowed.
- Times and ratios need rewording ("3 to 1", "9 am").
- Parentheses are for acronym definitions and short specifics. One pair per bullet at most.

Example:

> Before: Built on Cloudflare Workers with TypeScript: D1 as the system of record; R2 for receipts.
> After: Built on Cloudflare Workers with TypeScript, using D1 as the system of record and R2 for
> receipt storage.

## Words that read as AI or as filler

The build script warns on these. Replace each with a plain word or with the fact it stands in for.

- leverage, utilize, harness, unlock, elevate, foster, showcase, underscore, delve
- spearheaded, honed
- robust, seamless, cutting-edge, dynamic, pivotal, world-class, game-changing
- passionate, thrilled
- results-driven, results-oriented, detail-oriented, fast-paced, team player, proven track record
- landscape, realm, tapestry, testament
- moreover, furthermore, additionally
- "I am writing to express", "I am confident", "perfect fit", "ideal candidate"

When a posting itself asks for a trait from this list, show the trait with evidence instead of
repeating the adjective. Screening software weighs hard skills and titles far above adjectives.

## Patterns that read as AI

- **Groups of three everywhere.** "Designed, built, and deployed" in every bullet. Vary the count.
- **Identical bullet shapes.** Same length, same rhythm, same opening verb. Mix one-line and
  two-line bullets and change the verbs.
- **"Not only X but also Y"** and other balanced contrasts.
- **Abstract claims.** "Improved operational efficiency" says nothing. Say what got faster and by
  how much.
- **A summary sentence closing each paragraph.** End on the last fact.
- **Praise for the employer in general terms.** "Your innovative culture" could be sent to anyone.
  Name one true, specific thing or say nothing.
- **Inflated verbs.** Use the verb that describes what happened: built, wrote, ran, led, sold, cut,
  fixed, taught, closed, analyzed, audited, modeled.

## Resume text

- Bullets open with a verb. Past tense for finished roles, present tense for current ones.
- Bullets carry no first-person pronouns and end with a period.
- One or two lines per bullet. Rewrite a bullet whose last line holds one or two words.
- Numbers are digits with units ("$1.2M", "40%", "15 people"). Keep one style for thousands and
  millions across the page.
- The summary, when the field uses one, is two or three sentences without pronouns. Its last
  sentence names the exact role sought.
- Spell an acronym out once, with the short form in parentheses, then use the short form.
- Keep the user's own phrasing from the master wherever it is already plain. It is their voice.

## Cover letter text

- First person, as the candidate would speak to a respected colleague.
- Sentences vary in length. A short sentence after a long one reads as a person thinking.
- Every paragraph contains at least one detail only this candidate could write: a number, a named
  system, a moment. The detail comes from the master resume or from the user. Never invent an
  anecdote, a feeling, or a personal detail to add color.
- Contractions follow the field. Avoid them in finance, banking, law, and consulting. One or two
  are fine elsewhere.
- When `profile.md` names a voice sample, match its sentence length and formality.
- When `profile.md` lists words the user would never say, treat them as banned.

## Final read

Read the finished text line by line and ask of each sentence whether the candidate would say it out
loud to the hiring manager. Rewrite any sentence that fails.
